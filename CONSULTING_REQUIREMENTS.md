# Machine Learning Consulting Section Requirements

## Purpose

Add a concise consulting section that helps a prospective client quickly answer four questions:

1. Can Matt solve the kind of problem I have?
2. What concrete outcome can I expect?
3. Why should I trust him with the work?
4. What is the easiest way to start a conversation?

The section should present Matt as a technically rigorous, hands-on machine-learning engineer who can take an ambiguous data problem from evaluation through a practical implementation. It should not read like a generic agency page or an exhaustive list of tools.

## Target Customers

- Small and midsize organizations with valuable data but limited in-house ML expertise.
- Technical founders who need a prototype, feasibility assessment, or independent model review.
- Research teams that need production-minded help with scientific imaging, computer vision, or predictive modeling.
- Organizations whose existing model is inaccurate, difficult to maintain, or not producing useful business decisions.

## Customer Needs to Address

Prospective customers are primarily looking for:

- A clear assessment of whether machine learning is appropriate for their problem.
- Reliable data preparation, validation, and detection of data-quality risks.
- A measurable improvement over an existing baseline or process.
- Computer-vision or predictive-modeling expertise applied to their own data.
- An understandable prototype that can be evaluated before a larger investment.
- Reproducible code, documentation, and a practical path to deployment or handoff.
- Direct communication about uncertainty, tradeoffs, cost, and technical risk.

## Recommended Page Placement

Add `Consulting` to the primary intro links and place a dedicated consulting section near the top of `index.html`, after the featured redshift research and before the project collections. The research establishes credibility; the consulting section then translates that credibility into services.

The section should be collapsible to remain consistent with the portfolio's current interaction model, but the summary must clearly communicate commercial availability. Recommended summary: `Machine Learning Consulting`.

## Recommended Content Structure

### Opening Value Proposition

Use one short outcome-oriented statement:

> I help organizations turn complex data and imaging problems into validated machine-learning systems, from feasibility and model development through evaluation and technical handoff.

Avoid leading with frameworks, degrees, or broad claims such as “AI transformation.”

### Service Areas

Present three compact service cards:

1. **ML Feasibility & Strategy**  
   Evaluate the data, define a useful target, establish a baseline, and identify the lowest-risk path to a working system.

2. **Model Development & Evaluation**  
   Build and compare predictive models with reproducible experiments, appropriate metrics, error analysis, and clear benchmark reporting.

3. **Computer Vision & Scientific Imaging**  
   Develop segmentation, detection, feature-extraction, and image-analysis workflows for research or applied products.

### Engagement Types

Set expectations without publishing fixed prices:

- Focused technical review or model audit.
- Short feasibility study or proof of concept.
- Defined model-development project.
- Research collaboration or ongoing technical advisory work.

### Proof

Use existing evidence rather than adding unsupported testimonials:

- New benchmark results in photometric redshift estimation.
- Portfolio examples in segmentation, contouring, feature detection, classification, and regression.
- Links to the thesis, GitHub, and résumé.

Do not claim production deployments, revenue impact, client counts, or industries that cannot be substantiated.

### Call to Action

Primary button copy: `Discuss a Project`.

Link it to `mailto:mcferguson9@gmail.com` with a prefilled subject such as `Machine Learning Consulting Inquiry`. Supporting copy should tell the customer what to include:

> Briefly describe the problem, the data you have, and the outcome you want. I’ll reply with useful next steps and whether I’m a good fit.

The CTA should not promise a response time unless Matt wants to maintain that commitment.

## Visual and Interaction Requirements

- Match the existing warm background, gray borders, typography, and collapsible-section styling.
- Keep the section visually quieter than the featured research card.
- Use three equal service cards on desktop, stacking cleanly on smaller screens.
- Keep paragraphs short and scannable; avoid a wall of marketing copy.
- Use the existing button and focus styles.
- Preserve keyboard operation and visible focus states.
- Avoid stock imagery. Existing project evidence is more credible and visually consistent.

## Content and Trust Requirements

- Describe outcomes in plain language before technical methods.
- Distinguish a prototype or research result from a production-ready system.
- Mention confidentiality only if a real process for handling client data is established.
- Never request sensitive datasets by ordinary email; initial contact should discuss the problem at a high level.
- Avoid guarantees of accuracy or business performance.
- Make clear that project scope, deliverables, timeline, and ownership will be agreed before work begins.

## Acceptance Criteria

- A visitor can identify the three main services within ten seconds.
- The section explains both the work offered and the deliverable a client receives.
- One obvious CTA opens a pre-addressed consulting email.
- The section works at mobile, laptop, and 4K viewport sizes without excessive empty space.
- No claim exceeds the evidence already available on the site.
- Consulting content does not obscure or dilute the portfolio's research identity.

## Recommended First Version

The first implementation should include only the value proposition, three service cards, a short engagement-types line, one proof line, and the email CTA. A separate consulting page, pricing, scheduling integration, intake form, testimonials, and case studies should wait until there is enough real client activity to justify them.
